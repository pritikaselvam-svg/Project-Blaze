# Architecture B — Dual-Motor Rear-Drive

## Concept

Architecture B is a dual-motor rear-drive Formula Student Electric powertrain designed around an 80 kW total peak-power constraint.

The architecture uses independent rear-left and rear-right motors, enabling independent torque control and potential torque-vectoring strategies.

## Primary Design Objective

The primary objective is to investigate whether an electrically distributed dual-motor architecture can improve high-speed circuit performance while maintaining acceptable:

- Electrical efficiency
- Voltage drop
- Electrical losses
- Thermal behaviour
- Vehicle mass distribution
- Centre of gravity
- Yaw moment of inertia
- Energy consumption
- System complexity

## Initial Design Philosophy

The battery is positioned low and centrally within the vehicle envelope.

This is intended to provide a balanced mass distribution, low centre of gravity and low yaw moment of inertia while keeping the electrical paths to the rear power electronics reasonably short.

The final suitability of this position will be evaluated through modelling and simulation rather than assumed.

## Current Status

Architecture B has been conceptually defined.

Component-level validation, electrical calculations, thermal analysis and vehicle-level simulation remain to be completed.
